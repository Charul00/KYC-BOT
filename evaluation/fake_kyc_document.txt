Services Limited and its subsidiaries ("eClerx", "the Company", "we") collect,

SECTION 3: CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION PROGRAM (CIP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 REQUIRED IDENTIFICATION DOCUMENTS

For INDIVIDUAL customers, eClerx requires:

Primary Identity Documents (any one):
- Passport (valid, not expired)
- Aadhaar Card (12-digit unique identification number)
- Voter Identity Card issued by Election Commission of India
- Driving License (valid)
- NREGA Job Card duly signed by State Government official
- National Population Register Letter

Address Proof Documents (any one):
- Utility bill not more than 3 months old (electricity, telephone, water, gas)
- Bank account statement not more than 3 months old
- Letter from employer on letterhead
- Rent agreement registered with government authority

Financial Documents:
- PAN Card (mandatory for transactions above INR 50,000)
- Form 60/61 (for individuals without PAN)
- Income Tax Returns for last 2 years (for high-value transactions)
- Salary slips for last 3 months (for salaried individuals)

For CORPORATE/INSTITUTIONAL customers, eClerx requires:
- Certificate of Incorporation
- Memorandum and Articles of Association
- Board Resolution authorizing account opening
- List of Directors with their KYC documents
- Beneficial Ownership Declaration (for ownership > 25%)
- Audited financial statements for last 2 years
- GST Registration Certificate
- Latest annual return filed with Registrar of Companies

For FOREIGN NATIONAL customers:
- Valid Passport (mandatory)
- Visa documentation
- Work Permit or Residence Permit
- Foreign address proof (utility bill or bank statement)
- Home country identity document

3.2 DOCUMENT VALIDITY AND STANDARDS

All submitted documents must meet the following standards:
- Documents must be self-attested by the customer
- Photographs must be clear and recent (not older than 6 months)
- All documents must be in English or accompanied by certified translation
- Expired documents are not acceptable under any circumstance
- Certified true copies accepted only from gazetted officers, notaries, or bank officials
- Original documents must be presented for in-person verification

3.3 DIGITAL KYC (eKYC)

eClerx supports Aadhaar-based eKYC through UIDAI's Digilocker platform:
- OTP-based eKYC: Customer provides Aadhaar number and authenticates via OTP
- Biometric eKYC: Authentication via fingerprint or iris scan at authorized centers
- Video KYC (V-CIP): Live video interaction with trained eClerx officials
- Consent is mandatory and must be recorded electronically

eKYC Limitations:
- Maximum transaction limit for OTP-based eKYC: INR 1,00,000 per year
- Biometric-based eKYC: No transaction limit (subject to risk assessment)
- V-CIP applicable for full KYC completion remotely

SECTION 4: CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD) FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 RISK CATEGORIZATION

eClerx classifies customers into three risk tiers:

LOW RISK:
- Salaried government employees
- Individuals with regulated income sources
- Public sector undertaking employees
- Transaction values consistently below INR 1,00,000 per month
- Customers with established 3+ year relationship and clean transaction history

MEDIUM RISK:
- Self-employed professionals (doctors, lawyers, CAs)
- Small business owners
- Non-resident Indians (NRIs)
- Customers with moderate-value transactions (INR 1,00,000 – 10,00,000/month)
- First-time customers without prior banking relationship

HIGH RISK:
- Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and their family members
- Non-resident entities from high-risk jurisdictions
- Cash-intensive businesses (jewelers, dealers, real estate)
- Customers subject to adverse media or negative news
- Transactions above INR 10,00,000 per month
- Customers dealing in virtual assets/cryptocurrency
- Shell companies or complex ownership structures

4.2 SIMPLIFIED DUE DILIGENCE (SDD)
Applicable only to Low Risk customers:
- Basic ID and address verification required
- Reduced document set acceptable
- Annual review cycle (vs quarterly for high-risk)
- Self-declaration of income acceptable up to INR 5,00,000

4.3 STANDARD DUE DILIGENCE (SDD)
For Medium Risk customers:
- Full ID, address, and income verification
- Source of funds declaration required
- Semi-annual review
- Face-to-face verification or V-CIP mandatory

4.4 ENHANCED DUE DILIGENCE (EDD)
For High Risk customers:
- All standard documents plus additional scrutiny
- Senior management approval required for onboarding
- Source of wealth documentation mandatory
- Quarterly account review
- Ongoing transaction monitoring
- Third-party database screening (OFAC, UN, EU sanctions lists)
- Negative media check (World-Check, LexisNexis)

SECTION 5: POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEPs)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 DEFINITION

A Politically Exposed Person (PEP) is an individual who is or has been
entrusted with prominent public functions, including:

- Heads of State or government
- Senior government ministers
- Senior judicial officials (High Court, Supreme Court)
- Senior executives of state-owned enterprises
- Senior officials of political parties
- Members of Parliament or legislative assemblies
- Senior military officials (rank of Brigadier or above)
- Ambassadors and high commissioners
- Directors and senior management of international organizations

Domestic PEPs: Senior officials within India
Foreign PEPs: Officials from outside India
International Organization PEPs: Officials of UN, IMF, World Bank, etc.

5.2 PEP-RELATED REQUIREMENTS

- Immediate family members and close associates of PEPs are treated as PEPs
- All PEP accounts require approval from the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
- Continuous monitoring of PEP accounts is mandatory
- PEP status must be re-verified every 12 months
- Source of wealth and source of funds must be documented in detail
- Unusual transaction patterns must be reported to FIU-IND within 7 days

SECTION 6: ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING (AML) PROCEDURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 TRANSACTION MONITORING

eClerx employs an automated Transaction Monitoring System (TMS) with the
following alert thresholds:

Cash Transactions:
- Cash deposits or withdrawals above INR 10,00,000 in a single day
- Multiple cash transactions aggregating INR 10,00,000 or more in a day
- Cross-border wire transfers above USD 10,000 (or equivalent)

Suspicious Patterns:
- Rapid movement of funds in and out (round-tripping)
- Structuring transactions to avoid reporting thresholds (smurfing)
- Transactions with no apparent business purpose
- Sudden change in transaction pattern inconsistent with customer profile
- Transactions with sanctioned countries, entities, or individuals

6.2 SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTS (SAR/STR)

eClerx is required to file Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) with
FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit - India) in the following cases:
- Transactions involving proceeds of crime
- Transactions that may be linked to terrorism financing
- Transactions inconsistent with the customer's known business/income
- Unusual urgency or secrecy in transaction requests

Filing Timeline:
- STR must be filed within 7 working days of suspicion being raised
- Cash Transaction Report (CTR): Filed within 15 days of month-end
- No obligation to inform the customer that a report has been filed
- "Tipping off" a customer about an STR is a criminal offense

6.3 SANCTIONS SCREENING

eClerx screens all customers and transactions against:
- UN Security Council consolidated list
- OFAC SDN (Specially Designated Nationals) list
- EU Consolidated Sanctions List
- HMT (UK) Consolidated List of Financial Sanctions Targets
- RBI caution list
- INTERPOL notices

Frequency of screening:
- New customers: screened before onboarding
- Existing customers: screened in real-time for each transaction
- Full database refresh against updated sanctions lists: daily

SECTION 10: ACCOUNT TYPES AND SPECIAL CATEGORIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 MINOR ACCOUNTS

Accounts for minors (below 18 years):
- Natural guardian (parent) must be joint account holder
- Birth certificate of minor mandatory
- Guardian's full KYC documents required
- Account automatically reviewed when minor turns 18
- No debit card facility until customer turns 18

10.2 JOINT ACCOUNTS

- KYC for ALL joint account holders mandatory
- Survivorship clause must be clearly stated
- Either/Survivor or Former/Survivor operating instructions documented

10.3 NON-RESIDENT INDIAN (NRI) ACCOUNTS

Account types available:
- NRE (Non-Resident External) Account: Repatriable, held in INR
- NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account: Non-repatriable, held in INR
- FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) Account: In foreign currency

Additional documents required:
- Valid passport with valid visa
- Proof of residence abroad (utility bill or bank statement from foreign country)
- FEMA declaration form
- PAN card (mandatory for NRIs with Indian income)

10.4 DORMANT ACCOUNTS

An account is classified as dormant if:
- No customer-initiated transactions for 24 consecutive months
- Prior to dormancy classification, eClerx must:
  (a) Send 2 written notices to customer's registered address
  (b) Send email and SMS alerts
  (c) Attempt phone contact

Dormant account activation:
- Fresh KYC documents required
- In-person verification at branch or V-CIP

SECTION 11: BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 DEFINITION OF BENEFICIAL OWNER

A Beneficial Owner is any individual who:
- Holds 25% or more equity/ownership in a company
- Exercises ultimate effective control over the entity
- Provides the majority of funds or assets to the entity

11.2 BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP REQUIREMENTS

For all legal entities:
- UBO (Ultimate Beneficial Owner) declaration mandatory
- Ownership structure must be traced to natural persons
- Complex structures (more than 3 layers) require additional documentation
- Nominee shareholders must declare the actual beneficial owner
- Trust accounts: Settlor, trustee, and beneficiaries must all be identified

11.3 CORPORATE TRANSPARENCY

eClerx maintains a Central KYC (CKYC) record for all customers through:
- CERSAI (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction)
- KYC Registration Agencies (KRAs) as approved by SEBI
- Interoperability with CKYC registry for customer convenience
